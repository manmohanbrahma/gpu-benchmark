Enterprise AI coding on Kimi K3: why the backend network sets the size of the fleet

A company of 5,000 developers runs coding agents on Kimi K3 in FP8, served from HGX B300 servers. This model asks how many developers each GPU can serve at an interactive speed, and how that changes with the backend network: 8 × ConnectX-8 at 800G per server, 2 × BlueField-3 at 400G, 8 × 10G or 1 × 10G.

CX-8: about 1.8× the developers per GPUCX-8 lets one Kimi K3 instance span 8 servers. That spreads the experts thin enough to free memory for long coding contexts and still finish each step in time.

On a single pair of servers, CX-8 and BF-3 stay within about 5 to 15% of each other. The gap comes from scale: wide expert parallelism moves gigabytes between servers on every step, and only CX-8 has the bandwidth to keep going past 4 servers.

The workload: one coding-agent turn

Coding agents differ from chat in three ways that matter for the network. Prompts are long and mostly cached: each turn carries the repository context, the conversation and the last tool output, so besides the cached context the model processes about 7 new prompt tokens for every token it writes. The contexts fill GPU memory: 64k tokens of Kimi K3's attention cache and recurrent state is about 1.2 GB per session. And the agent waits for every turn, so a slow token rate is paid 30 times per task.

Where the network enters

Spread the experts wider

Kimi K3 has 896 experts and 2.7 TB of expert weights in FP8. On 2 servers each GPU holds 170 GB of them; on 8 servers, 43 GB. Each step reads less, and the freed memory holds about 2.4 times as many 64k sessions per GPU.

Pay for it on every layer

Each token visits 16 experts, mostly on other servers, in all 92 MoE layers. With 504 sessions generating on 4 servers that is 2.9 GB per server per step, in a step that has about 40 ms to finish.

Stop where the network runs out

On CX-8 the transfer is about a tenth of the step, so going to 8 servers keeps paying off. On BF-3 it nearly fills the step at 4 servers and gets worse at 8. On 10G, expert parallelism across servers does not work at all, so each pair of servers runs as a 2-stage pipeline.

Cache moves and the prefill handoff are small next to a 20 to 30 second turn on the RDMA fabrics, and the model charges them on 20% of turns. They matter on 10G, where every move costs a large fraction of a second, and they grow with context length.

Where CX-8 does not change the answer

  • Small teams on one pair of servers. CX-8 and BF-3 stay within about 5% from 8 to 190 active sessions, and within 15% for a single developer. 10G keeps up only with one or two sessions at a time.
  • Relaxed batch work. For overnight refactors, CI code review or test generation, 15 tokens per second per stream is enough. At that target BF-3 on 4 servers needs the same 96 GPUs as CX-8 on 4 servers; only the 8-server CX-8 instance does better.
  • If FP8 is not required. Kimi K3 ships in MXFP4 and fits on one server. Single-server replicas take the backend network out of the request path, at the cost of the wide-instance gains above.

Method and limits

  • Model: Kimi K3 per its model card: 2.8T parameters (often quoted as 2.6T), 104B active, 93 layers (69 KDA, 24 gated MLA), 896 experts with 16 per token. FP8 weights, about 2.9 TB.
  • Step time is the larger of HBM reads (touched experts, attention weights, KV cache, KDA state at 6.4 TB/s per GPU) and FP8 compute (4.5 PF at 45%), plus 45 µs per layer, plus network time left after 70% overlap on the RDMA fabrics. Attention is tensor-parallel inside each server; experts are spread across all servers of an instance, with DeepEP-style one-copy-per-server sends.
  • Workload: 64k-token context, 4k new prompt tokens and 600 output tokens per turn, 5 s of tool time, 30 turns per task, closed loop. 20% of turns need their cache from another server; the cheaper of moving it or recomputing it is charged.
  • Fabric figures per server: CX-8 about 680 GB/s effective and 20 µs per exchange; BF-3 about 85 GB/s and 28 µs; 10G over TCP at 7 and 1.1 GB/s. The 10G options use a 2-stage pipeline with one 14 KB activation per token per hop.
  • Assumed, not on the model card: 0.3 GB of KDA recurrent state per session. Not modelled: speculative decoding, tail latency, switch congestion, and the larger failure domain of an 8-server instance, where one server failure stops 64 GPUs.
  • Latency calibration follows a 2 × 8 H100 study of tensor and pipeline parallelism across InfiniBand. These are planning estimates; a concurrency sweep on real hardware at 4 and 8 servers is the check that matters most.
